Somewhere Beyond The Sea

Memoir

★★★★★

Summary

I'm Glad My Mom Died is Jennette McCurdy's memoir about growing up under the weight of a controlling, abusive parents while navigating early fame, disordered eating, and the impossible expectations placed on young girls in the entertainment industry. It's raw, darkly funny, painfully honest, and written with a clarity that only comes from someone who has done a lot of work to understand the shape of their own traums. McCurdy traces her childhoos, her rise on Nikelodeon, her complicated relationship with her mother, and the long, messy process of reclaiming her life. It's a book about survival, identity, and the strange grief of losing someone who hurt you.

Thoughts

This one had been on my radar for ages, so when I saw it on the shelf while popping into the library to pick up a hold, had to grab it.

Now I have to buy it.

Jennette McCurdy and I are the same age, and while our upbringings were wildly different (thank fuck), so much of her life was deeply relatable. The constant pressure to stay thin and the awareness of your body as something to be looked at and evaluated at such a young age is such a Millenial experience. She's also turned her traums into being really fucking funny, which...yeah. Another Millenial experience.

I laughed, I cried, I shook my head. I felt things I didn't expect to feel. I tore through it in 24 hours. There was something incredibly healing about reading the story of another woman who grew up under impossible expectations and is now clawing her way back to herself.

It's sharp, it's devastating, it's weirdly comforting, and it's absolutely worth the hype.

Massive TW for descriptions of eating disorders and various kinds of abuse. But it's 10000% worth the read.
