Mad Ship

Fantasy

Finished on Jan 22, 2026 • ★★★★★

Summary

The second book in the Liveship Traders trilogy deepens every thread introduced in Ship of Magic. The stakes rise at sea and on land as the Vestrit family gractures, Vivacia's sentience grows more complicated, and the political tensions of Bingtown begin to boil. The narrative widens, the world expands, and the emotional core of each character sharpens into something raw and compelling. It's a story about identity, legacy, and the cost of freedom - for ships, for families, and for entire nations.

Thoughts

Second books in trilogies are always my favourite, and this one absolutely delivered. The pacing is relentless in the best way. Tearing through adventure after adventure, with a final 200 pages that feel like being swept into a maelstrom. Kennit remains one of the most fascinating characters in the series. The way his charmed bracelet acts as a hateful mirror to his inner self is brilliant and unselling, and I love how Hobb uses it to explore the tension between self-mythology and truth. Wintrow, Kennit, Etta, and Vivacia could carry an entire book on their own. Their dynamic is so layered and dynamcically charged that I find myself looking forward to their chapters. Malta, meanwhile, is frustratingly relateable; a perfect portrait of a girl growing into herself in the most chaotic circumstances imaginable. This book is adictive, smart, and full of character development. The subplots are rich and purposeful, and you can start to see how the different storylines will eventally collide. It's the kind of book that makes you want to immeditely pick up the next one (and I did).
