Ship of Destiny

Fantasy

Finished on • ★★★★★

Summary

The final book in the Liveship Traders trilogy brings every thread together in a sweeping, emotional conclusion. The fates of Bingtown, the Vestrit family, the serpents, and the liveships converge in a story about memory, transformation, and the cost of survival. Dragons rise, alliances fracture, and long-buried truths finally surface. Hobb masterfully balances action, character development, and thematic depth to deliver a satisfying end to an epic saga.

Thoughts

This was a hard group to say goodbye to. Hobb's ability to make every character, even the ones you hate, feel painfully real is on full display. Kennit's storyline was especially difficult to read, but ultimately one of the most compelling arcs in the trilogy. The way his past shapes him, the way memories defire empathy and the capacity to care, and the way trauma echoes through generations is all handled with such care and nuance. His ending is tragic, inevitable, and thematically perfect. The liveship and dragon plotline was another highlight for me. It's a heartbreaking and realistic look at how humans convince themselves they are superior to everything around them, especially when profit is involved. The moral complexity of that storyline hit hard. A brilliant, emotional, and deeply satisfying conclusion. BRB as I gather the rest of the Elderling series (only 11 books to go).
